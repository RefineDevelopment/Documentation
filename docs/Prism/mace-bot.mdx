# Mace bot

Mace practices aerial approaches, pearl catches, elytra movement, and timed smash attacks.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Spawn | `/spawnbot mace medium` |
| Configuration | `plugins/Prism/bots/mace.yml` |
| Practice focus | Wind-charge launches and mace smashes |
| Controls | Shift + right-click the bot |

:::info Configuration paths
Current generated files group numeric launch/smash/chain controls and humanization under `ADVANCED.MACE`. Technique toggles remain under `BEHAVIOR.MACE`. See [Configuration](bot-configuration.mdx#advanced-settings).
:::

The mace brain uses `MaceMovementController` for timed movement and projectile
sequences. It coordinates movement and pearl decisions with native combat execution. Attack damage, shield disabling, fall distance, and item cooldowns
remain subject to the server's normal combat handling.

New bots use Normal difficulty, with elytra takeoffs and offensive pearl catches
enabled. The kit includes an elytra and wind charges. Elytra approaches start
at 8–24 blocks; wind launches handle closer approaches. During takeoff setup,
the bot stops advancing once it is within launch range and looks at the target's
eye height. Airborne movement steers and brakes over the target.

To enable both techniques in a customized `bots/mace.yml`, set
`BEHAVIOR.MACE.PEARLS.OFFENSIVE` and `BEHAVIOR.MACE.ELYTRA.ENABLED` to `true`.

## Movement and pearl decisions

- **Jump launches:** the bot jumps first and only fires the downward launch
  charge after it is airborne and moving upward.
- **Alien charges:** an elytra launch fires straight down, flicks upward to
  45 degrees, and holds that climb direction for four ticks. A gliding bot
  descending near solid ground can also attempt this boost when its opponent
  is still more than four blocks away horizontally.
- **Dive commits:** the bot leads the opponent's movement and considers its
  own forward motion before ending its glide over the target. It restores the
  chestplate it swapped out before returning to falling melee combat, including
  the existing axe-to-mace stun slam.
- **Pearl catches:** nearby targets favor a straight upward pearl; targets at
  least six blocks away horizontally favor an upward/forward pearl. The wind
  charge follows on the next tick and is placed across the pearl's predicted
  movement segment. Both launches account for the shooter's inherited motion
  using the server's relative-projectile-velocity setting. Prediction applies
  pearl gravity and drag before movement, matching Paper's tick order. If the
  alignment or item use fails, the bot can retry for up to four ticks after
  throwing the pearl. Elytra catches keep the glide active.
- **Wind-charge elytra launches:** the gliding bot stages a charge, waits for
  the native deflection window, and punches it downward. The combat planner
  advances only after confirmed explosion knockback. Pearl catches use their
  own projectile intercept sequence and do not wait for a ground teleport.
- **Apex extensions:** an ordinary wind launch can transition into a glide near
  its apex when more horizontal distance remains. The movement controller ends
  the glide over the target before the combat planner resumes the smash.
- **Offensive grapples:** the bot can pearl toward a rising or gliding opponent
  above it, leading their motion and aiming within the lower part of their
  current bounding box. This also gives it a follow-up opportunity if its
  wind-charge counter launches an opponent. Flight time is recalculated with
  pearl drag and gravity; unreachable throws and trajectories crossing blocks,
  unloaded chunks, or the world border are rejected.
- **Pearl dodges:** an incoming mace dive can trigger a pearl away or sideways.
  The bot checks for a nearby solid surface, standing clearance, the world
  border, separation, and hazardous floor materials before attempting the
  throw. A wind-charge counter remains available when no pearl dodge is found.

Consumables must exist in the bot's inventory and be off cooldown. Pending
pearl catches and grapples prevent overlapping sequences. Pause, shutdown,
target changes, and ground-pressure controls cancel pending movement inputs.

## Verification

Run the existing suite and compile with:

```powershell
.\gradlew.bat test :plugin:compileJava --console=plain
```

The existing suite does not simulate these projectile and elytra interactions.
Check the following on a Paper development server before tuning the timings:

1. At medium range, confirm jump, downward charge, and upward elytra flick occur
   in order. Repeat with no elytra or with wind charges on cooldown.
2. Descend near ground while the target is out of melee range; confirm the boost
   spends one charge and does not immediately repeat.
3. Glide toward moving targets; confirm glide cancellation and chestplate
   restoration occur before a smash or stun slam.
4. Compare close upward catches with distant horizontal catches. Confirm one
   pearl and one charge are consumed, and flight continues during elytra catches.
   Repeat while ascending, gliding forward, and moving sideways; also check with
   Paper's relative projectile velocity disabled.
5. Launch upward and glide as the opponent; check grapple aim against both
   standing and gliding hitboxes, including intervening walls.
6. Dive toward the bot on open ground and near obstacles; check dodge direction,
   landing, and fallback counters. Pearl flight uses a ballistic trajectory, so
   terrain screening does not guarantee the eventual landing point.
7. Pause, retarget, or despawn during each sequence; confirm no delayed movement
   input fires afterward and swapped chest equipment is restored.

Other ideas in the summary, such as airborne healing, deliberate heal baiting,
enemy-pearl tracking, backstab routes, and double-breach scheduling, are outside
this movement-focused pass.
