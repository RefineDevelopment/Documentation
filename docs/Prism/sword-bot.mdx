# Sword bot

Sword practices melee spacing, confirmed-hit combos, sprint resets, and timed critical hits.

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Spawn | `/spawnbot sword medium` |
| Configuration | `plugins/Prism/bots/sword.yml` |
| Practice focus | Combos, sprint resets, and critical exchanges |
| Controls | Shift + right-click the bot |

Select **Sword** in `/spawnbot`, or use `/spawnbot sword hard`.
The profile dialog controls difficulty, sprint resets, critical exchanges,
opponent tracking, and practice resistance. The default equipment preset uses Protection III diamond armor, a diamond sword, and an empty offhand. Inspect the inventory view for the equipment on your active bot.

Sword uses the diamond sword sprite. DiamondSMP uses a cobweb sprite and keeps
its existing survival combat behavior. Integrations can identify the new bot
with `xyz.refinedev.prism.api.bot.BotType.SWORD`; existing enum identifiers are unchanged.

## Combat behavior

- **Opening:** use an adaptive engagement bait near attack range, then close
  after an apparent missed swing or a short timeout. Hit selection is reserved
  for the eight ticks after the bot lands a confirmed critical hit.
- **Combos:** confirmed sprint hits trigger a brief W-tap, S-tap, or sideways
  sprint reset before another charged attack. Strafing and spacing continue
  between hits. Two unanswered hits trigger a short S-tap escape.
- **Critical exchanges:** retaliate while falling after an incoming crit, and
  keep moving forward after a crit to maintain contact. This forward pressure
  attempts the tutorial's no-KB exchange through normal movement; it does not
  remove or scale server knockback.
- **Double crits:** after a confirmed punish crit in a close exchange with a
  recent incoming crit and a non-sprinting opponent, prepare a second jump as
  soon as grounded and the weapon has begun recovering. Attack only on descent
  with sufficient charge.
- **Jump resets:** use a cooldown-limited grounded jump after a recent incoming
  hit in a close hit-select or double-crit exchange. Ordinary combos favor
  backing away rather than repeated jumps.
- **Falling sprint hits:** occasionally choose a sprint knockback hit during
  descent in a close crit chain. Sprinting is enabled for that attack; normal
  critical attacks disable it.
- **Aim and reach:** aim just below the opponent's eye position, following their
  crouching pose. A ray from the bot's actual gaze must intersect the target's
  bounding box within the difficulty's reach, capped at three blocks, with
  line of sight. Aim error and missed attacks scale with difficulty.
- **Latency:** at a reported ping of 150 ms or more, favor close crit exchanges
  over repeated knockback combos. This is a heuristic based on reported ping,
  not a prediction of individual packet delivery.

The brain uses the existing fake-player movement, vanilla jump and attack
hooks, and confirmed damage events. It respects pauses, PvP disablement,
weapon cooldowns, and target changes. Its movement and attacks also pass
through the existing learning recorder.

## Adaptive engagement and tempo

Before a new exchange, Sword chooses among six timed approaches:

- **Double S-tap:** retreat, briefly release movement, then retreat again.
- **S-tap + strafe:** retreat first, then move sideways near engagement range.
- **Jump bait:** show grounded forward pressure, wait for the opponent to jump,
  then delay the bot's own jump by two to three ticks. The attack still waits
  for descent, charge, and legal reach. This requires critical hits enabled.
- **Crouch bait:** approach, crouch, release, and crouch again; a descending
  opponent can prompt a duck just before engagement. Crouching uses the normal
  pose/hitbox update and the equipped sneaking-speed attribute, including Swift
  Sneak, so it changes both eye height and closing speed.
- **Normal retreat:** hold backward briefly before turning toward the opponent.
- **Aggressive first hit:** immediately press forward, sprinting when enabled.

Tap lengths, pauses, and strafe directions vary. The same technique cannot be
chosen twice in succession, and after three defensive attempts the bot switches
to aggression. Every bait has a timeout; a passive opponent cannot keep the bot
retreating indefinitely. Existing combo escapes and airborne crit sequences take
priority over selecting another bait.

Each bot keeps a bounded in-memory history for up to 16 opponents during its
lifetime. It tracks attempts, nearby main-hand swings, inferred misses, first
hits, and successful punishes. Early swings favor distance baits, apparent aim
misses favor strafing, jump habits favor crouching, and reactions to grounded
pressure favor delayed jump baits. Recent results gradually change selection
weights, with 25% uniform exploration among eligible alternatives. There is no
cross-session model or guaranteed success percentage.

Swing and damage events are reconciled over a short grace period before grading.
A miss means an observed nearby swing without confirmed damage to this bot;
it is an inference, not access to the opponent's client crosshair or intention.
Invulnerability windows are excluded from miss rewards. The profile displays
the current opponent's counts after results settle. Pausing, losing the target,
or losing line of sight releases crouch and cancels the active sequence.

## In-server checks

After building, verify on a Paper development server with two players:

1. Spawn Sword through the picker and command; verify its profile, diamond
   equipment, empty offhand, and diamond sword icon. Verify DiamondSMP's cobweb.
2. Start combat and check charged sprint hits and brief sprint resets. Land
   two unanswered hits to check the backing escape.
3. Trade close crits and observe punish crits, occasional double crits, and
   falling sprint hits. Crouch near maximum range to check head tracking.
4. Disable sprint resets and critical exchanges independently, then pause and
   resume. Ensure the bot abandons its previous exchange when retargeted.
5. With a genuinely high-ping opponent, check that it favors close crit
   pressure. Tune timing against live movement if needed.
6. Approach repeatedly, swing early, jump, or aim off to the side. Observe the
   six engagement states, changes in the profile's outcome counts, varied
   choices, and aggression after defensive attempts. Verify crouch changes the
   visible pose and actual movement speed and is released when paused.
